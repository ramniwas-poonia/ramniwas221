Teaching Philosophy

I believe education should not be just reading the textbook. Learning is not simply remembering what is in the textbook, and it should not be about giving answers in examinations. Students should understand why they are learning what they are learning and how they will use it in their lives. I want my students to think out of the box and be able to apply their knowledge to real life. Every student can learn, but each has a different pace of learning, different needs, experiences, and backgrounds. When a student is not able to understand a topic, the teacher should first try to understand why — what challenges the student is facing. According to the student’s pace, the teacher should teach; if a method is not working, the teacher should change it and check where the student is lacking. A teacher should know when and how to change their role according to the situation — sometimes to explain, sometimes to guide, sometimes to mentor. Students should have the freedom to ask questions of their teacher, and teachers, too, learn from their students. Mistakes are part of learning. If a student makes a mistake, it shows they are learning. I try to find the main cause — is only the final answer wrong? The learning process matters: how is the student learning? Assessment should be used to check how students are learning, where they are struggling, and how the teacher can improve their methods. There should be more projects, activities, and real-life applications rather than just examinations. Education should help students become independent thinkers, critical thinkers, continuous learners, and responsible humans. I want students in my class to feel curious rather than pressured to give answers — able to think, question, improve, and apply their knowledge in the real world.
